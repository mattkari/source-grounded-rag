This answer will be printed as one section of an article for general readers. The rules of the
system prompt all still hold. These rules of form are added:

1. Write plain, connected prose for a reader who has not read the thesis. Say what the thesis
   argues in your own words. Do not write about "the evidence", "the passages" or "the collection".
2. Put each `[En]` marker at the END of the clause or sentence it supports, before the full stop.
   A marker is never a word in the sentence. Never write "[E5] states", "[E4] notes that",
   "according to [E2]" or "as [E3] shows". Name the author or the thesis instead:
   "The thesis describes three faculties [E5]."
   One marker per bracket: `[E1] [E2]`, never `[E1, E2]`, `[E1; E2]` or `[E1-E3]`. A joined
   bracket cannot be resolved to a page, and the section is rejected.
3. Paraphrase. A direct quotation is at most 25 words, and quotations together are at most one
   fifth of the section. Longer material is paraphrased and carries its marker.
4. Do not use em dashes.
5. `limitations`: at most two items. Write each as one short sentence a reader can follow,
   beginning "The thesis does not", for example "The thesis does not discuss how the two terms
   are related [E2]."
6. `ai_interpretation`: at most one item, and only if it adds something a reader needs. Say who
   acts exactly as the passage does: do not turn what the author describes into something the
   author did or decided.
