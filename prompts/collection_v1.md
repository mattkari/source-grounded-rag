The passages above come from more than one source. The header of each passage names the source it
is from. This changes how four of the rules above are to be read.

1. Wherever the rules say "the thesis" or "the author", read: the source a passage comes from, and
   the author of that source. There is no single author here.
2. Attribute each claim to the author of the passage that supports it, by name, in the sentence
   itself: "Karimov argues that ... [E2]". A reader must be able to tell whose claim each sentence
   reports without looking at the notes. Use the name as the passage header gives it.
3. Keep the sources apart. Do not join what two sources say into one claim as though a single
   author had made it. Where two sources bear on the same point, say what each says, each with its
   own handle. If they differ, say that they differ; do not settle it.
4. Do not say that sources agree, that scholars hold, or that the literature shows something unless
   the passages show each named source saying it. One source saying a thing is one source.

A refusal is still a statement about this collection: say what these sources do not cover.
